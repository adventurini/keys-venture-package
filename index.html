02

The roadmap

How a Founding Builder would take this from zero to revenue.

The plan

Build the rails while rates are high. Own the wave when they break.

Renters are renting longer than any cohort in history — which means the deepest verified payment histories ever assembled are accumulating right now. Keys captures them before the first-time-buyer dam breaks. Every phase below is gated: we earn the next investment by passing the last gate.

Asset-light, alwaysWe own readiness data and the handoff — never homes, never loans, never balance-sheet risk.
Borrowed rails firstPartner for bureau furnishing and lending. Build only what's proprietary: the score, the alert, the lease graph.
Lea is the productKeys isn't a new app. It's new superpowers for the assistant 41M renters already know.

Phased roadmap

Five phases. Each one cheap to test, expensive to skip.

0

Validate demand

Weeks 0–6

The question: do renters actually opt in when it's free and one tap away? Concierge-everything — no engineering beyond a landing page.

What we ship

  • Waitlist page inside Renter Hub + post-lease-signing email to recent matches
  • Concierge pilot: 50 renters manually enrolled via a white-label rent-furnishing partner
  • 20 renter interviews on the "thrown-away rent" pain and score concept
  • Partner LOI shortlist: bureau furnishers, 2–3 lenders, agent networks

Gate to Phase 1

  • ≥3% opt-in from post-lease email cohort
  • ≥35% of pilot completes payment connection
  • Furnishing partner terms signed
Revenue: $0 — by design
1

Ship the rails

Months 2–5

The wedge: tri-bureau rent reporting, free for every Apartment List renter, with up to 24 months of history backfilled. The single highest-value, lowest-novelty thing we can ship — the rails everything else rides on.

What we ship

  • Rent reporting v1 via furnishing partner (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion)
  • Keys Score v1: payment streak + credit band + metro affordability, refreshed monthly
  • Lea integration: monthly score update in the channel renters already read
  • Enrollment built into the lease-signing flow — the moment of maximum trust

Gate to Phase 2

  • 5K enrolled renters
  • ≥50% still enrolled at day 90
  • ≥18% open their monthly score update
Revenue: $0 — building the asset
2

The crossover engine

Months 5–9

The moat: the rent-vs-buy crossover alert. We already publish the most-cited rent data in the country — pointing it at each renter's actual numbers is something neither Bilt nor Zillow can replicate, because neither holds both sides.

What we ship

  • Crossover model per metro: live mortgage rates vs. our rent data vs. your rent
  • Lea alerts: "owning just got cheaper than renting for you" — the message that makes Keys famous
  • Property partner pilot: Keys as a free retention amenity at 3 A-List communities
  • Readiness milestones + savings pace nudges

Gate to Phase 3

  • ≥15% engagement on crossover alerts
  • Directional renewal lift at pilot properties (full statistical proof not required)
  • 1 lender partnership signed for Phase 3
Revenue: $0 — earning the right
3

Monetize the handoff

Months 9–15

The flip: today, our highest-LTV customer event — a renter buying a home — earns us nothing. Phase 3 turns graduation day into the best unit economics in the company. A buy-side referral is worth thousands; a lease referral is worth hundreds.

What we ship

  • Pre-approval marketplace: 2–3 lender partners, soft-pull first, inside Lea
  • Vetted buyer's agent network in 3 launch metros (high crossover-alert density)
  • Closed-loop attribution: enrolled renter → alert → pre-approval → close
  • Mortgage-readiness packet: verified rent history formatted for underwriting

Gate to Phase 4

  • 50 pre-approval starts
  • First 5 attributed closings
  • CAC-free funnel confirmed (enrolled base only)
Revenue: first dollars — lender + agent referral fees
4

The dam breaks

Months 15+ · rate-cycle catalyst

The payoff: when rates drop, years of pent-up first-time buyers move at once — and whoever holds their payment history, readiness scores, and trust owns the wave. We'll have spent two years becoming the obvious front door.

What we scale

  • Agent + lender network to 25 metros, prioritized by crossover density
  • Down-payment savings boost with a partner bank (still asset-light)
  • Readiness data products for lender partners — the B2B second act
  • Full-circle loop: Keys buyers become Apartment List landlords and referrers

What success looks like

  • Keys attribution on 0.1% of partner-metro first-time purchases
  • Referral revenue/enrollee trending toward lease referral revenue/renter
  • Enrollment cited unprompted by ≥5% of new renters in launch metros
Revenue: scaled — the second engine

Unit economics

Why graduation day beats lease day.

~$300
Today · per lease referral

What a matched renter is worth now. One transaction, then we wait years to see them again — or never do.

$2–4K+
Phase 3 · per attributed closing

Buy-side agent referral (a share of commission) plus lender referral fees — on a customer we already acquired. Marginal CAC: approximately zero.

$0
Cost to the renter, forever

Free is the strategy. Enrollment is the moat — every reported month deepens data nobody else has and loyalty nobody else gets.

The honest math: Keys only needs a small single-digit percentage of enrolled renters to transact through the handoff for the program to out-earn what those same renters generate in lease referrals — because each conversion is worth roughly 10x and the audience is already paid for.

Why now

The window is open. It won't stay open.

Three clocks are running at once.

01

The underwriting door is open. GSE programs now count verified rent history toward mortgage eligibility. The rails are sanctioned — someone just has to build the on-ramp at scale.

02

The competition is moving. Bilt has partnered with the nation's largest wholesale lender to run this exact play from the payments side. They need renters to switch how they pay. We don't — we're already there at lease signing.

03

The cohort is historic. High rates mean renters are renting longer and accumulating deeper payment histories than ever. When rates break, the wave goes to whoever holds the data. That's a 12–24 month head start that can't be bought later.

Phase 0 costs six weeks and a landing page.

The downside is one quarter of one builder's time. The upside is the second revenue engine — and the reason renters never want to leave the platform, right up until the day we hand them their keys.
